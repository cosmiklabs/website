Made to be revised

Keep the idea.
Change the details.

Tectra builds with connected parts and named dimensions. A door belongs to an opening. A roof meets its walls. Those relationships give the next revision something to work with.

  1. 01

    Ask for the change you mean

    Widen a doorway, change a roof or adjust a chair’s proportions. Direct the result in words instead of editing individual vertices.

  2. 02

    Make the relationships explicit

    Parts share dimensions and declared requirements. Changes can carry through the connections, with checks that show where a requirement fails.

  3. 03

    Keep the decision yours

    Inspect a proposal before accepting it. Compare revisions, discard a change or return to an earlier accepted state.

How it works

Describe the space.
Shape what comes next.

A conversation moves the work forward. Review keeps you in control.

  1. 01 / Describe

    Start with an idea

    Explain what you want to make. Include dimensions, references and relationships when they matter.

  2. 02 / Inspect

    Look at the proposal

    Explore the geometry, select a part and inspect the requirements checked against the structure.

  3. 03 / Refine

    Ask for a revision

    Point to what should change. The agent develops the model around your next request.

  4. 04 / Decide

    Accept it when ready

    Compare the proposed change, then accept or discard it. Earlier accepted revisions remain available.

Checks you can inspect

Know what was checked.

Declared rules make requirements visible: a minimum gap, a part’s fit or a route through a structure. Findings identify the rule, its measured result and where to look.

A rule passing means that declared requirement passed. It is not a certification of real-world safety or suitability.

Questions

Before you begin.

Can I use Tectra today?

Tectra is in active development. Authoring conversations, proposal review, revision history, materials and export are being developed together. Public access is not available yet, and no launch date or price has been announced.

What can I make with it?

Tectra is for constructed 3D forms: objects, furniture, vehicles, machines, interiors and buildings. It makes individual structures rather than complete environments. The examples on this page explain that workflow; they do not establish compatibility with a particular production pipeline.

Do I edit the model by hand?

You direct the work through conversation and inspection. Select a part, describe a change and review the resulting proposal. Tectra’s workflow is not a traditional mesh editor.

What do the checks mean?

Checks evaluate requirements declared in the structure, such as dimensions, clearances and relationships. They help you inspect an intended design. They do not establish building-code compliance, fabrication readiness or physical safety.

Will the exports work with my tools?

GLB is the portable export path. The result depends on the receiving application’s support for the included materials and extensions. The shader package requires a compatible integration. Tell Cosmik which tools you use so that the requirements are understood.

Who makes Tectra?

Tectra is developed by Matthew Downs at Cosmik, an independent software company. Tectra has its own identity and product direction.
